# Sonlet — first-round identity concepts

Brief: Sonlet. Speak locally. Build freely. A lightweight on-device speech toolkit for Android.
Audience assumed: Android developers and independent builders; secondary audience: people using embedded speech input.
Personality: compact, capable, approachable, independent, precise.
Applications: repository header, documentation, Android demo app, favicon, monochrome UI.
Constraints: exact Sonlet casing; readable at 16 px; no reliance on colour; keep tagline outside the core logo.
Decision-maker: project owner. Scope: three reviewable concepts, not a production identity kit.

Research: the bundled developer-tools library has 295 entries; containment, negative space and monoline geometry recur. Avoid default microphones, equalizer bars, clouds, shields and generic code chevrons. Wave marks and flight birds are crowded territory; a bird must have a distinctive silhouette rather than a familiar flying-bird pose. References studied for construction, never traced: Framer, Pagekit, npm, Patreon.

Word map: Sonlet → sound, small unit, S; local → cell, inside, contained, pocket; build → module, opening, join; freely → open boundary, wing, movement. Intersections: small speech unit / split S; local core / open cell; small voice / perched bird.

Longlist, scores out of 5 (clarity / distinction / simplicity / relevance / small size):
1. Split S: two opposing speech turns form a modular S. 5/4/5/5/4 — SELECT.
2. Local cell: an open enclosure protects a single independent core. 5/4/5/5/5 — SELECT.
3. Pocket bird: a compact, upright songbird with an open folded wing. 4/4/4/4/4 — SELECT.
4. Speech seed: one seed grows into a voice aperture. 3/3/5/4/4.
5. Local brackets: opposed square brackets hold a speaking dot. 4/2/5/5/5.
6. Unplugged loop: an S circuit breaks at an input terminal. 3/3/4/4/3.
7. Cursor voice: a cursor becomes the terminal of the S. 3/3/4/5/3.
8. Let ligature: custom l/e/t joins express a reusable toolkit. 3/4/3/4/2.
9. Home tone: a rounded room contains a sound notch. 3/2/4/4/4.
10. Three modules: three offset tiles become a speaking profile. 3/3/3/4/3.

Construction, before drawing:
A: two 180-degree circular hooks with 32-unit bands, 16-unit central air gap, diagonal inner terminals; rotated partners on a 256 square.
B: one 32-unit rounded square enclosure, right opening 64 units high, independent 48-unit core; shared corner geometry.
C: a compact upright bird built from circular head/body contours and a diagonal folded wing cut; no eye or miniature details.
Wordmark: custom geometric Sonlet paths, manually constructed; no external font or live text in the logo assets. Presentation captions use system sans.

All SVGs here are review concepts. Production colour, reversed optical corrections, platform icon exports and guidelines follow direction selection. No trademark clearance performed.

Refinement loop 1: inspected the full concept image and light/dark, scale, blur, mirror and rotation tests. All core silhouettes survived 16 px; the bird silhouette is less descriptive there. The bird head was too rectangular; softened its rear upper corner with a 32-unit radius. Tightened the horizontal symbol–wordmark gap by 12 units across all directions. Original meaningful iterations preserved.

Refinement loop 2: rendered and inspected the revised overview, the bird scale ladder (16–256 px), white/reversed backgrounds, blur, reflection, rotation, circular avatar and app contexts; compared all three beside four reference marks. Split S remains the recommendation for its direct name association and balanced modular geometry. B has the cleanest tiny silhouette but may read as a C; C has the warmest personality but the least explicit tiny-size reading. Bird's asymmetric bounds are intentional: the beak extends beyond the main body, which carries most visual mass.
Validation: all six v2 SVGs parsed successfully; no live text, embedded raster, filters, masks or clips. Automated audit: A/B symbols 100; C symbol 99; lockups 99/100. These are structural heuristic scores, not originality or legal clearance. The black artwork requires a white/reversed treatment on dark backgrounds. Production minimum sizes and reversed geometry remain for the selected direction.
